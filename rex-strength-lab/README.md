# Rex Super-Strength Lab

A physics simulator for Rex, an indestructible, tireless dinosaur, and his boyfriend Ollie, a bull-man who is just as strong and just as indestructible. Set the strength anywhere from the world-record human (511 kg deadlift) to infinite. On his own, Rex can walk, lift, throw, jump, stomp, punch, clap, sprint or pull, or just stand there at any density (the Weight tab). With Ollie he can arm-wrestle, wrestle, play catch, or test the unstoppable force against the immovable object. Each tab shows what the physics does to the two of them, the object, the ground and the air. Every number on the page is either sourced or labelled an estimate.

Strength is how strong he is; **effort** is how hard he chooses to push. They want to be seen as heroes, so the page opens on the least disruptive effort: the gentlest push that still does what each move is for. Each move also shows its **courtesy ceiling** (the most effort before something nearby comes to harm, and which hazard sets it) and **not-so-heroic presets**, each set by a hazard (rattle the windows, crack the plaster, break windows across the street, break the ground, flatten the houses next door, level a block), up to all-out. The **Walk** tab shows what being near them is like: how hard their footsteps shake the ground, how far away people feel it and a glass of water ripples, and their footprints. **Their powers**, one panel in the sidebar, gathers every comic-physics option, since all of them are their absorption at work, used on purpose: the muscle model (comic book or real muscle), Bolted to the planet (one switch each for Rex and Ollie), five **default powers** that are on unless the reader turns them off (canon: comic-book physics is their default; they choose when not to use it), and switches that each suspend one piece of real physics (silent strike, soft landing). The default powers: **Air resistance absorbed** (a 0–100% slider; they take back that share of the air's drag on what they throw or hold, so at 100% it flies drag-free), **Things keep their form** (nothing they hold or throw is broken by them or the air), **Unbreakable footing** (the ground under them never breaks, but the push still goes in, so it shakes as real physics says), **They take their own recoil** (the push back from their own moves, the stop of a catch included, goes into their source, the endless ocean, not into the planet, so they stay planted and none of it shakes the ground) and **Safe passenger** (someone they throw or catch feels only a manageable push, spread through them, at the launch, in the air and when they land; the landing's energy is taken back, and its push still reaches the ground and shakes it). Each labels the results it changes ("no drag: their power", "held together by their power", "the ground holds: their power"). The energy they absorb can go back to the endless store they draw on (canon) or, as a what-if, become growth.

The page is laid out around Rex: the sidebar holds his strength dial, his size and density, the ground, and Their powers; his tabs are the centre of the page. The two-player tests sit in their own **Rex + Ollie** element below his tabs, with Ollie's own settings at its top. It starts closed and works nothing out until it is opened (the page remembers whether it was open).

Energies are set beside a **ladder** of 58 sourced reference energies, from the energy to lift a 100 g apple one metre (about 1 J) to the first black-hole collision ever detected (about 5 × 10⁴⁷ J): a multiple of at most about 10× of the nearest one below, or a share of at least about 10% of the nearest one above, and only a rough figure (“roughly 3×”) for a reference uncertain by more than 2×. Past either end the page says the energy is beyond the ladder.

## The default bodies

Rex is 10 ft (3.048 m) tall by his owner's ruling. Ollie measures 3.06 m to his horn tips, which is 10 ft within measuring error.

Their mass is also by ruling: each weighs **3,000 kg** in all, 300 kg of it Rex's tail (ruled on 13 Oct 2026; before that Rex was 2,700 kg plus a tail of about 307 kg measured from the picture, 3,007 kg). Ollie's tail is about 11 kg, so about 2,989 kg of him is body. At 1,060 kg/m³ that is 2.83 m³ each.

The rest comes from their reference picture (`assets/duo.jpg`), which on its own measured less volume:

- Scale: Rex is 790 px from crest tip to floor, so each pixel is 3.86 mm.
- Volume: each body is cut into 1-pixel slices, and each slice is treated as an ellipse. Its front-to-back depth is its visible width times a depth ratio taken from US Army body measurements (ANSUR II). Limbs are sliced across their own axis. Only one arm of each is fully visible, so both arms are sized from that one. Rex's tail includes the part hidden behind his hips. Rex comes out at 2.55 m³ and Ollie at 2.53 m³, tails included. Three independent methods agreed within 3% once each one's errors were corrected. The ruled mass needs both bodies about 11–12% bigger than this at the same density, most likely deeper front to back than the borrowed ratios; side views will test it.
- Density: 1,060 kg/m³. Lean bodybuilders measure 1,081 kg/m³ with lung air removed (Fry, Cisar & Housh 1987); about 2% comes off for the air in the lungs and gut.
- From the same picture: facing area 4.4 m² for Rex (tail trailing) and 4.5 m² for Ollie, and one foot's sole 0.31 m² (Rex's paws) and 0.15 m² (Ollie's boots).

In the engine these are the `rex` and `ollie` builds (`BUILDS` and `DEFAULT_BODIES` in `src/engine.js`): volume 0.099947 × height³ (from the ruled mass), with areas scaling as height². The ruling, the picture method and its checks are in `research/final-duo.json` (`rex_ollie_picture_build`, `density_bodybuilders_fry1987`).

The strength dial is calibrated on a separate, human-sized body: 2 m, 180 kg, 1,000 kg/m³. At record-human strength it reproduces the shot put, pitching and sprint records in both muscle models. You can pick it from the **Calibration body (2 m, 180 kg)** chip under Size. The real-human feats (deadlift, shot put, Bolt's sprint, and so on) run on it when you press Simulate, because at those strengths the 10 ft, 3,000 kg Rex can't get up. The density chips include **His build (default)** at 1,060 kg/m³ and **Water** (1,000 kg/m³, fresh water at 4 °C, the calibration body's density).

## Files

- `src/engine.js`: the physics. Bodies and builds; two-body push (relativistic constant force, or Hill force-velocity with an implicit integrator); footing and ground limits; drag flight over a round Earth with Kepler arcs above 150 km; the Weight tab's self-gravity, PREM fall through the Earth and settling; arm wrestling, wrestling, the game of catch and the collide test; Kinney-Graham blast, Gutenberg-Richter quakes and Collins et al. craters.
- `src/app.html`: the page. Every sentence a reader sees is generated in the `bay*` functions from engine numbers.
- `src/ladder.js`: the energy ladder's wording (no physics): names the nearest rung and gives a multiple, a share, a rough figure, or beyond the ladder. Runs in the browser and in Node, so `test/ladder.js` calls it directly.
- `src/static-data.js`: the assumption cards and the height references.
- `research/final-*.json`: the sourced facts behind every number (humans, fiction, objects, environment, density, duo, presence, energy). Each fact carries its status, sources and notes. `final-presence.json` (270 facts: step forces, ground shaking, elephants, thresholds, the water glass, comic switches, growth) was first assembled by `research/merge_presence.py` from the compiled research of this brief (each topic researched, checked by a skeptic who re-fetched the sources, and compiled with every correction applied; the compiled topic files are in the session's history scratchpad). Since review round 1 it is edited by hand and is the source of truth: re-running the merge would undo the review fixes. Status rule: a number we read off a published plot, or converted, is `derived` (the display says so); `verified` is reserved for numbers as the source states them.
- `research/final-energy.json`: the energy ladder's facts (140: the 58 rungs, their inputs, and alternates left off the ladder). Researched in seven topics (an audit of the old rungs, then six energy bands), each checked by a skeptic who fetched every source and compiled with the corrections applied, then merged by hand (`research/reviews/gui-c-research.json`). A rung's page phrase is its fact's `page_short`.
- `build-data.py`: picks the facts the page uses and writes `src/data.js` with status and sources; the `LADDER` list there picks the rungs. It fails if a fact key is in two research files or two neighbouring rungs are more than 100× apart.
- `research/reviews/`: the review records (see Review history).

## Their own recoil goes to their source (BRIEF-canon.md pull request 2)

Canon (CLAUDE.md, 14 Oct 2026): "*They take their own recoil* (RT A7): the push back from their own moves goes into their source, not into the planet, so they stay planted and the ground isn't shoved." Until this pull request the lab sent that push through their feet into the planet, where it shook the ground. This pull request builds items 2, 3 and 7 of `BRIEF-canon.md`.

**What changed.** With They take their own recoil on, the push back from his throws, punches, stomps, pulls and shoves, from both throws and both catches in the game of catch, from the partner toss and from the winner's swing in an arm-wrestling pin goes to the endless ocean. He stays planted, as before; none of it reaches the ground, so none of it shakes the ground. A pull's own steps are the move, not recoil, so they still push on the ground, and with the power on they are the pull's shaking source (review round 2). Throw, Stomp, Pull, the shove, the Wrestle throw, the game of catch and the partner toss say how much momentum went there (the default car throw: about 22,000 kg·m/s). A punch's push back comes back when his arm stops (net zero), so Punch gives a figure only where the power saves a lurch; an arm-wrestling pin gives one (the net of both arms' push backs, which they share in one source) where the power keeps a quick pin's slam whole in the ground, with Unbreakable footing on. The header reads "Indestructible · never tires · unlimited", and "momentum is never absorbed" is gone from the page, the cards, this README and the engine's comments; the cards list canon's cases where momentum is absorbed.

**Decisions** (the brief left these open; the review rounds checked them):
1. The push back goes to the endless ocean; the engine reports it (`recoilAbsorbed`, and `recoilAbsorbedT` and `recoilAbsorbedC` for the game of catch's thrower and catcher) only with the power on. No ground push and no shaking come from it, and Unbreakable footing's weak-ground cap no longer applies to it.
2. With They take their own recoil and Bolted to the planet both on, their own push back still goes to the endless ocean, not through the bolts. Bolted stays the setting for being pushed by someone else.
3. His feet carry only the weights: with the power on, the load on his feet in a throw, a catch and the partner toss is his weight plus what he holds. Holding a load or a partner up is weight, not recoil, as on the Lift tab.
4. A catch (canon: "a catch is absorbed ... unless he chooses otherwise"): with the catcher's power on, his stop goes to the endless ocean in every outcome where the object reaches him. What he catches never makes him slide or carries him off, so his arms alone must stop it, and a catch his slide used to share can become a slam. (A throw that reaches him as plasma isn't caught: it is an incoming hit, Never knocked back's case, pull request 4, and it still carries him off; the page says so there.)
5. Stomp: the upward push on his body goes to the endless ocean. The foot's blow on the ground is the move and stays whole; nothing cancels it, so the old "upper bound" caveat goes with the power on. With it off it stays: bolted, the bolts' upward pull partly cancels the blow; standing, his standing foot unloads by the part of the push that doesn't lift him (all of it when his weight holds him down), which does the same.
6. Punch: the push back goes to the endless ocean during the stroke and comes back when his arm stops, so nothing net goes anywhere.
7. Pull: the rope's pull back goes to the endless ocean, bolted too (decision 2), so he can pull with all of the strength he uses. He still walks with the load, though: his feet move his own body with at most his legs' push, and on friction at most his grip too. His legs push his own body level with at most the strength he uses, the Sprint tab's push-off rule (review round 3's re-check; round 3 had used 1.4×, the Weight tab's rule for legs that carry his weight up, so towing a car he sped up faster than he can sprint). Bolted removes the grip limit, not the muscle limit (review round 3; round 2 had left his bolted steps uncapped, and before that a bolted pull with the power on moved at the pair's pace). So the pace is the smaller of the load's ((his drive − the resistance) ÷ the load's mass) and his own body's (his feet's push ÷ his mass), and the rope pulls only as hard as keeps the load at that pace. Bolted and pulling hard, a load lighter than about 3,000 kg (his own mass, less a little for the load's resistance) goes at his own body's pace; a heavier one sets the pace itself. At his own body's pace a towed load speeds up exactly as fast as his bolted sprint (296 m/s² all-out at the default dial), but over 10 m the pull is still a little quicker, because it has no leg-speed limit and no air drag on him: a car pulled all-out at the default dial takes 0.26 s and he ends at 77 m/s, where his unloaded bolted sprint, held to 61 m/s by how fast his legs can move, takes 0.27 s (Known limits). His own steps are the move, not recoil: their push on the ground (his own body's momentum over the pull, the engine's `walkPush`) is real, and with the power on it is the pull's shaking source. On friction his grip caps it, so the shaking is small; bolted only his legs do, and a quick, hard pull can crack plaster nearby. With the power off the lab counts no pull's shaking, as on `main`. The load's rolling or sliding resistance presses on the ground too, a steady drag the lab doesn't count.
8. Shove: the shover's push back goes to the endless ocean. A bolted partner's bolts still hand the planet the push (with Unbreakable footing on and the shover's power off). At unlimited strength into a bolted partner (review round 1), the shover stays put, standing or bolted, and his source takes the unbounded push back; on real rock only the rock around the partner's bolts tears out and the partner leaves at just under light speed; where the ground under the partner holds, nothing moves (an unlimited push against a hold without limit has no physical answer: pull request 4's case).
9. Wrestle throw: the thrower's push back goes to the endless ocean; the thrown partner's landing stays real.
10. Arm wrestling: with the winner's power on, the pin's swing pushes back into his source, so with Unbreakable footing the slam reaches the ground whole at any swing time. The presses, the table and standing up are pull request 4.
11. The game of catch and the partner toss: each throw and each catch go to the endless ocean, Rex's toss and his catch of Ollie too.
12. The weak-ground rule keeps its role for catches with the power off, landings, a bolted body and Unbreakable footing alone.
13. Collide, the jump, the sprint, the clap and growth are unchanged here (Collide's charger is pull request 4).
14. The new figures appear only with the power on, so with every power off every engine result is exactly what it was (the 15,541 powers-off cases in `test/powers.js`).

## Known limits

- Ground shaking (Walk tab and the restraint checks) comes from an elastic-ground model fed with force pulses. It matched one drop test within about 2× to 60 m, one recorded person walking past at 53 m on sandy ground (Steinmann 2025) within about 3× (the stiffness of that sand is unknown, so firm to loose), and elephant footfalls roughly; read it as uncertain by about 3–5×, more past 60 m (where it falls no faster than the measured r^-1.3). The ground constants for each surface are our picks. Lake ice and a steel deck are not modelled. Indoors, floors and walls can make shaking 2–4× stronger.
- Their gait is people's gait scaled to their size (equal Froude number) from an estimated hip height (0.45 of height); the heel strike is the one measured at people's preferred speed, used at every pace, so pace barely changes the shaking, and the fastest pace (Froude 0.5) is past the step-rate fit (0.09–0.37); they stride heel-first like people (canon), but how Rex's paws and Ollie's boots cushion a step is not canon. The soft-footed factor (0.51 of an ordinary heel strike: the cut people make walking quietly at their normal speed) is an estimate.
- Footprints in sand are scaled from one measured elephant print, from the step's landing peak; a stamp's peak is past the 350 kPa the rule was fitted to, so its print is extrapolated (the tile says so). Standing still (the Weight tab) uses the same print rule at his standing pressure; in sand and gravel his feet plunge deeper only past the pressure the elephant's sand bore with a shallow print (350 kPa at its foot's width, scaled with width; more for gravel). That onset is our estimate: it rests on the researchers' modelled 350 kPa for the elephant's forefoot; force-plate data suggest nearer 181 kPa, in which case sinking starts sooner than shown. Clay uses the Walk tab's rule; no measured clay print anchors it. The quick-load bearing the moves use for sand (37 kPa at 0.316 m, the textbook value) is unchanged (owner's decision, 12 Oct 2026: leave it for now), so it sits about 5–10 times below what the elephant's sand bore: jumps, stomps and pushes on sand give way sooner than standing does.
- Thresholds: rattling and losing one's footing have no standard; ours are estimates (1 mm/s; 1 g up and down at the step's frequency). 7.6 mm/s (repeated steps) and the single-event curve (12.7 mm/s for slow jolts, rising with frequency to 50.8 mm/s at 40 Hz, USBM RI 8507 Figure B-1) are safe limits for plaster, not the point where cracks start. A pulse lasting T is taken at frequency 1/T, our estimate.
- Effort and the courtesy ceiling check seven hazards near him (a sonic boom, an air blast over 100 Pa, ground shaking past the plaster safe level, ground breaking under him, a crushed object, a thrown person pressed past ride limits, his own recoil shoving him past the nearest people: the lurch during the push plus the coast, where Catch counts only the coast). An air blast counts only when something moves past Mach 1 or makes a fireball (or, in Collide, when the hit is quicker than sound crossing the damage zone); slower releases are thumps, and the Punch and Clap tabs say so. In soil (sand, gravel, clay), sinking in is a footprint, not broken ground; only a crater counts, and for a jump that means over 1 MJ into the ground, our line (no source gives one).

          How the numbers are made

          • Same shape. At a new height H, weight is W = W₀(H/H₀)³ and each girth is G = G₀(H/H₀). If you set a weight, girths follow G = G₀√((W/H)/(W₀/H₀)). That keeps the shape of each cross-section.
          • Why this law. In ANSUR II men the chest follows it almost exactly (exponent 1.02 on √(W/H)). Between soldiers the waist grows faster (1.44), and the calf (0.84), forearm (0.76) and neck (0.70) grow slower. ANSUR II has no body-fat data. By Forbes's relation (Hall 2007), roughly 60-70% of the weight difference between people is fat (estimate, from data on women). So those per-girth exponents describe soldiers who differ mostly in fat. The page offers them only as the "Average soldier" mode.
          • How good it is. On ANSUR II men the law's scatter (1 SD) is 3.0% for the chest and 5.7% for the waist. At a fixed BMI it is accurate to about 1% for height changes of 10% or less. From 1.8 to 2.2 m the error grows to about 2.7% (waist) and 3.5% (neck).
          • Height cubed is a scaled copy. Real adults of different heights differ more like height squared (exponents 1.8-2.3). So the page also shows the same-BMI weight when you change height.
          • Missing girths come from a template build that has them: same sex, similar body type, closest weight for its height. They are scaled the same way and labelled derived, with the template named.
          • Beyond human data. The highest BMI of anyone with measured girths here is 43.45 (an ANSUR II man). Above it, girths are model estimates with no human check. The same holds for heights more than 10% outside the ANSUR II range of 1.41-1.99 m (below 1.28 m or above 2.19 m), because the scaling law is checked only for height changes of about 10%. The largest flexed arm in ANSUR II is 49.0 cm.
          • FFMI is fat-free mass over height squared. The scale plots it at the build's own height. For men between 1.55 and 2.05 m the page also gives Kouri's normalised value, FFMI + 6.1 × (1.80 m − height); the abstract prints 6.3, and the difference is at most 0.05 units in that range. Kouri's sample was men, so women get no normalised value. Each line on the scale says its basis: median lines are unadjusted; the Kouri and Trexler lines are height-adjusted values converted back to this height (Trexler used its own slope, 2.943).
          • Reverse mode. Estimate A is the ANSUR II log-linear regression on height and the seven girths (men: RMSE 1.63 kg, 1.91%; women: 1.50 kg, 2.19%). Estimate B sums stacked circular frustums built from the same inputs, times a calibration constant of about 1053 kg/m³ (error SD 2.31% men, 2.78% women). B can be adjusted for body fat with Siri densities (fat 0.900, fat-free 1.100 g/cm³). ANSUR II has no body-fat data, so the reference fat for that adjustment is an assumption.

TOPIC: where extreme muscle stops a body working. The chart will mark, by 2D geometry on drawn front-view silhouettes whose widths are scaled up at fixed height, three functional limits: (a) the arms can no longer hang down by the sides, (b) the thighs touch all the way to the knee, (c) the hands can no longer reach the face. Find sources that make these definitions defensible: (1) soft-tissue approximation as the end-feel limiting elbow flexion and knee flexion (Norkin & White "Measurement of Joint Motion", Kapandji, Magee "Orthopedic Physical Assessment", AAOS norms); normal active elbow flexion range (about 140-150 degrees) and what limits it. (2) Functional elbow range for daily tasks, especially hand-to-mouth, eating, reaching the face and head (Morrey, Askew & Chao 1981 J Bone Joint Surg Am 63:872; Sardelli et al. 2011 J Bone Joint Surg Am; Oosterwijk et al. 2018 review; Raiss et al. 2007); give the elbow flexion angle each task needs. (3) Evidence that very large muscle mass reduces range of motion (studies of bodybuilders' or powerlifters' flexibility and joint range, e.g. Chang, Buschbacher & Edlich 1988 "Limited joint mobility in power lifters"; any study measuring elbow flexion or shoulder adduction in bodybuilders); and shoulder adduction or the arm-against-torso contact (lat spread). (4) Inner-thigh contact: any anthropometric source on thigh contact or the "thigh gap" in men, and the standing stance used in anthropometry (ANSUR II standing posture: heels together or feet apart?). (5) Joint geometry needed: elbow flexion stops when forearm and upper-arm soft tissue meet; give sources or models that compute maximum flexion from limb cross-section (e.g. in biomechanics or ergonomics, CAD human models such as Jack or RAMSIS, or soft-tissue collision in animation papers). Recommend precise, citable definitions and thresholds for (a), (b), (c), say what each ignores (3D shape, tissue compression, posture), and label every threshold as an estimate.

## Known limits

- Ground shaking (Walk tab and the restraint checks) comes from an elastic-ground model fed with force pulses. It matched one drop test within about 2× to 60 m, one recorded person walking past at 53 m on sandy ground (Steinmann 2025) within about 3× (the stiffness of that sand is unknown, so firm to loose), and elephant footfalls roughly; read it as uncertain by about 3–5×, more past 60 m (where it falls no faster than the measured r^-1.3). The ground constants for each surface are our picks. Lake ice and a steel deck are not modelled. Indoors, floors and walls can make shaking 2–4× stronger.
- Their gait is people's gait scaled to their size (equal Froude number) from an estimated hip height (0.45 of height); the heel strike is the one measured at people's preferred speed, used at every pace, so pace barely changes the shaking, and the fastest pace (Froude 0.5) is past the step-rate fit (0.09–0.37); they stride heel-first like people (canon), but how Rex's paws and Ollie's boots cushion a step is not canon. The soft-footed factor (0.51 of an ordinary heel strike: the cut people make walking quietly at their normal speed) is an estimate.
- Footprints in sand are scaled from one measured elephant print, from the step's landing peak; a stamp's peak is past the 350 kPa the rule was fitted to, so its print is extrapolated (the tile says so). Standing still (the Weight tab) uses the same print rule at his standing pressure; in sand and gravel his feet plunge deeper only past the pressure the elephant's sand bore with a shallow print (350 kPa at its foot's width, scaled with width; more for gravel). That onset is our estimate: it rests on the researchers' modelled 350 kPa for the elephant's forefoot; force-plate data suggest nearer 181 kPa, in which case sinking starts sooner than shown. Clay uses the Walk tab's rule; no measured clay print anchors it. The quick-load bearing the moves use for sand (37 kPa at 0.316 m, the textbook value) is unchanged (owner's decision, 12 Oct 2026: leave it for now), so it sits about 5–10 times below what the elephant's sand bore: jumps, stomps and pushes on sand give way sooner than standing does.
- Thresholds: rattling and losing one's footing have no standard; ours are estimates (1 mm/s; 1 g up and down at the step's frequency). 7.6 mm/s (repeated steps) and the single-event curve (12.7 mm/s for slow jolts, rising with frequency to 50.8 mm/s at 40 Hz, USBM RI 8507 Figure B-1) are safe limits for plaster, not the point where cracks start. A pulse lasting T is taken at frequency 1/T, our estimate.
- Effort and the courtesy ceiling check seven hazards near him (a sonic boom, an air blast over 100 Pa, ground shaking past the plaster safe level, ground breaking under him, a crushed object, a thrown person pressed past ride limits, his own recoil shoving him past the nearest people: the lurch during the push plus the coast, where Catch counts only the coast). An air blast counts only when something moves past Mach 1 or makes a fireball (or, in Collide, when the hit is quicker than sound crossing the damage zone); slower releases are thumps, and the Punch and Clap tabs say so. A throw past Mach 1 counts the energy the air takes in flight and his own recoil past Mach 1 as a blast from him (all of it in one place: an upper bound). In soil (sand, gravel, clay), sinking in is a footprint, not broken ground; only a crater counts, and for a jump that means over 1 MJ into the ground, our line (no source gives one). Ground shaking is not modelled for Sprint (the footstep model covers walking paces only), Clap (two hands push on his chest equally and oppositely) or Punch (a punch into air leaves the ground no net push: the arm's stop hands it straight back within the time his legs spread a push over), and the page says why. A pull's shaking counts only with They take their own recoil on: the rope's pull back then goes to the endless ocean, and the shaking is from his own steps (his own body's momentum over the pull: capped by his legs, at most the strength he uses, as in a sprint, and on friction by his grip too). With it off the lab counts no pull's shaking, as on `main`: his footing takes the rope's pull back, a steady load over seconds, or his bolts do, over the pull, which all-out can take only a fraction of a second (0.31 s for a car at the default dial; 2.1 s for a locomotive, and longer at a low dial), and his steps push too, so a quick bolted pull may shake the ground more than the page says; the page says so. A power is credited in the effort box only where the hazard happens with that power off (tested all-out): Safe passenger with a thrown person pressed past the ride limits; They take their own recoil with his recoil shoving him back and, on Throw, with broken ground under him (Unbreakable footing off) and with the ground shaking from his own push (only where that push, not the landing, cracks plaster with the power off; it fires only in narrow cases, such as a semi thrown at 30° at a 10⁶ kg dial on concrete or asphalt, where with the power off his push, not the slow landing, is what cracks plaster; for most throws the landing cracks plaster at a lower effort first). The two-player tests share one effort, clamped to what they need to stand and walk, and offer the same not-so-heroic presets, judged on what each test does (5% search tolerance); their ground shaking is modelled only for pushes Unbreakable footing sends through their feet; with They take their own recoil on, each one's own push back (the shover's, the thrower's, the catcher's stop, the partner toss's toss and catch) goes to the endless ocean instead, and the plaster preset says why it finds nothing (with the powers off, a thrown partner's landing is the only shaking source, as before). The game of catch doesn't judge the shaking where the object lands on or around the catcher (too big to catch) or hits the ground (a throw past 1% of light speed from more than about 12 km), nor a fireball's hit on him; there the page says the lab doesn't judge it, not that nothing shakes the ground (review round 2).
- The effort searches walk a log grid (two points a decade, then seven more inside the bracket where a hazard first appears) and bisect to 0.5% (5% in the two-player tests). They stop early only when the move comes out the same at two efforts and all-out. They run in the background after a 40 ms budget; one probe runs the whole move once, so for heavy real-muscle throws and the game of catch a probe can still block the page: up to about a second for a solo move, 2–3 s for a real-muscle game of catch (there is no Web Worker). Two-player searches whose probes take longer than a render's budget start in the background, so the page first shows 'working it out'. Dragging the effort slider on Throw at unlimited strength in real-muscle mode can also stall for 0.5–1 s at some positions (the push's implicit integrator takes up to 600,000 steps there). While a search runs, the page shows all-out, labelled 'working it out'. A hazard that switches on and off within a narrower window can still be missed; when the effort shown falls inside such a window, the readout says the hazard happens now though the search found no start for it. Known cases: real-muscle throws, where the engine keeps whichever of two pushes throws a hair faster, so the grip on the object flickers past its limit over narrow bands of effort; and a real-muscle jump at huge strength (about 10⁸ kg), where two pushes give the same jump to a part in 100,000 and the engine's choice between them (one breaks the ground) flickers with effort; fixing that choice would change all-out results, which must match the earlier engine exactly.
- The passenger limits judge a person thrown or caught with Safe passenger off. They are ride-design limits for seated, supported people (worst axis); nobody has measured them for someone held in hands. Real-muscle throws are judged by the push's peak, comic ones by its average. Air drag in flight is judged against the held 1.5 g limit. With Safe passenger off, a thrown person's landing is reported as a fall height, with no injury threshold, and the courtesy ceiling doesn't count it (its shaking counts). With it on (the default) the launch, the drag, the catch and the landing are spread through them and count as harmless, by canon, not by any measured limit; the landing's energy is taken back, but its push is real and keeps the person's own quick stop (about their size over the landing speed), so it shakes the ground as a real landing does (pull request 3, decision 4). It doesn't cover heating in flight: where Things keep their form is off and the flight would burn them, the page doesn't call them unharmed (on Throw and in the game of catch). Taking back the landing's energy doesn't take back its force: past what the ground bears under the downward part of its push over the person's whole facing area (straight down from a landing at about 100 m/s on the concrete slab, 20 m/s on loose sand, 2.7 km/s on granite; faster for a slanting landing, about 120 m/s at 45° on the slab; estimates, `PASSENGER.landGround`), the ground gives way where they land. The lab doesn't size that hole or count it as harm (as for any thrown object's landing, only its blast and shaking count), and its shaking figures past that point are rough. On loose sand the person throw's courtesy ceiling at the default dial (plaster, a landing at 26 m/s) is already past what the sand bears (23 m/s at that angle). On ice, which has no ground-wave model, nothing counts the person breaking through: at a 10⁹ kg dial the courtesy ceiling (the boom, a landing at about 340 m/s) is past what ice bears (about 170 m/s at 45°, 140 m/s straight down). The landing's shaking (`landShake`, older than pull request 3) treats the whole momentum as a downward pulse, so it overstates the shaking of a slanting landing, and it now sets the person throw's courtesy ceiling (at the default 45° the downward part is 0.71 of the whole).
- Air resistance absorbed: the share taken back goes to the endless ocean (energy and momentum); the ocean figures are the drag the air would have taken along the path actually flown. The Throw tab's blast near him is (1 − share) × what real air takes from the same throw (one more flight at the full drag; all of its energy if real air never brings it down, an upper bound, or past 1% of light speed, where real air turns all of it into a fireball), one rule on both sides of 1% of light speed, so a 50% throw's blast from its flight is half the 0% one. That is the blast near him. A reach longer than the stretch where the air takes the energy counts all the air took within it: past 1% of light speed a throw the air stops within metres to a few km (half of it within fastAir's dHalf, the full-drag half length divided by the share of drag that still acts) reaches as far at 90% as at 0%, so the brief's 'half at 50%' holds near him but not for the far reach. The same holds below 1% of light speed for a supersonic throw: a far reach counts what the slowed throw gave the air within it (at most its own take). The Throw tab says which figures the blast uses. (On the whole flight the slowed object gives the air much the same energy as at 0%, spread over a longer path; we judge the blast where it starts, near him, where the share of the drag that acts is what counts.) The game of catch counts what the air takes from the slowed flight between them (it already carries the share): all of that flight is near them. Throws past 1% of light speed fly a straight line. The text gives the air's whole-path take from a relativistic snowplow (the swept air moves with it and momentum is kept), with the object sweeping air at that share of the rate; the game of catch's fireballs use the same rule. In the ground's frame, close to light speed even a small share of a long column of air takes almost all of its energy over the whole path: the All-Star baseball meets about 10,000 times its own mass of air on a level path, so at 50% the air still takes more than 99.9% of its energy along the way, though the blast near him is half the 0% one. (Something that sweeps little air, the Great Pyramid thrown straight up just past 1% of light speed, gives the air about 52% of the 0% take over its path; 84% for a level throw.) With any of their powers on, a supersonic throw also counts his throwing hand's own air (their own bodies meet real air; past the speed of sound it booms too): over the throw it drives the air in its path (about 0.14 kg for the 10 ft Rex) at the release speed. A piston at Lorentz factor γ does γ² m v² of work on the air (an upper bound, since the hand speeds up through the throw), capped at the work of his push; where the cap applies, the page says that in real air his hand's own drag would hold the throw far below that speed. His foot does the same in a stomp with Unbreakable footing on (the impact's energy is taken back, the foot's air isn't), and with any power on a supersonic jump counts what the air takes from his own flight. With all four powers off the lab counts as it did before them, without the hand (the slider's read-out says so at 0%). With Things keep their form on, at any share, a throw past 1% of light speed that reaches him arrives whole inside its plasma sheath, whatever share of its energy the sheath holds, and is caught with its full momentum (the lump's rest mass counts the swept air and its heat; round 4 removed a 1% line that flipped the result). The drag taken back goes to the endless ocean, so the object keeps its speed (it flies faster than in real air); it doesn't feed growth. At 100% a level throw past orbital speed is solved as a closed Kepler orbit and reported as an orbit. Every very fast throw that leaves (the Throw tab, the Wrestle throw, the partner toss straight up) gets the same text: the gravity bend of its path (at most twice the Newtonian figure, for relativity) and where it goes. Leaving the solar system takes at least about 16.7 km/s from the ground, going the way the Earth moves; below that it ends up on its own orbit around the Sun, and between that and about 72.8 km/s it depends on the way it goes.
- The game of catch at 100% absorbed: a throw past 1% of light speed flies the straight chord between their hands. Past about √(8 R h), about 12 km for hands 2.7 m up, that chord runs underground, so the throw hits the ground instead of reaching him. With any power on the same holds at any share: below 100% the air it meets turns to plasma around it and rides with it, so it hits the ground there with all of its energy (kept whole or not). A catch that fast keeps momentum (worked out from the throw's energy, since near light speed its speed rounds to c; unbounded at infinite strength). With They take their own recoil on (canon, 14 Oct 2026: a catch is absorbed unless he chooses otherwise) his stop goes to the endless ocean and he is never carried off; with it off, unless his footing holds, he is carried off with it (his footing's push over a stroke that short is negligible), and his own body meets real air, so the pair's ride heats the air near the catch (the snowplow fireballAt uses below 100%) and counts as blast; with him gone there is no round trip. The thrown hand's air, the throw's push into the ground (Unbreakable footing with They take their own recoil off), and a throw that hits the ground (energy, crater at the hit point's angle, quake) are counted as on the Throw tab. Pre-existing and left as it is (with the powers off every result must match `main`): below 100%, the game of catch aims a fireball throw with the Earth's curvature term of the wrong sign, a little above level instead of a little below; with every power off, whether a fireball reaches him past 12 km also comes from that aim (a straight line couldn't); with any power on, the chord test above replaces it.
- With They take their own recoil on, his own push back goes to the endless ocean: nothing of a throw's push, a shove, the thrower's push or the catcher's stop in the game of catch, or the partner toss's toss and catch reaches the ground, so a throw shakes it only where it lands, and a throw that never comes down shakes nothing. With it off and Unbreakable footing on, the ground takes his push at his feet, as before. With both off, the lab counts only the landing's shaking for a throw, as before, not his push into the ground at his feet; the same goes for the partner toss and the two-player pushes. A jump's push-off counts only with Unbreakable footing on (it is the move itself, not recoil). A pull's ground shaking is his own steps' push, counted only with the power on (the rope's pull back goes to the endless ocean): on friction his grip caps it (a car pulled all-out at the default dial: 0.011 mm/s at 5 m); bolted only his legs cap it (at most the strength he uses, as in a sprint), and all-out at the default dial a car's steps may crack plaster out to about 1.6 m and rattle windows out to about 20 m. With the power off his footing caps the rope's pull, a steady load over seconds, far too little to shake the ground (at a high density the rope gets only a small part of his footing's push; the rest moves his own body); a bolted pull with the power off is left out, as on `main`. The load's rolling or sliding, a steady drag over the whole pull, is never counted. Two more pushes count with the powers on: a bolted partner's bolts in a shove (the whole push, a body width away) and, in Collide, the charger's own take-off (his jump's push-off or his stomp-launch's foot), with his Unbreakable footing. So turning those powers off can lower the shaking shown even where nothing breaks. With Unbreakable footing on and They take their own recoil off, the throw's push the ground keeps is the object's momentum less his own recoil: the follow-through hands his arm's momentum back within the time his legs spread the push over.
- Things keep their form covers their grip, the launch, its own weight while held, heating and the air; every other collision is real. The brief's test (Everest hits a bird intact and breaks on the Moon) can't be run in the engine, which has no thrown-object collisions besides the landing.
- Unbreakable footing: it holds the ground only where real ground would break, by the ground hazard's own rule (a crater, a hole of 1 cm or more in hard ground, sinking past what it bears); a dent, or a footprint in soil up to the one the Walk tab allows (sand and gravel: the print where the ground starts to fail, 90 mm for the 10 ft Rex in sand; clay: none), is real and its energy goes into the ground as before. A stomp that would sink deeper keeps that print and its energy is taken back (all of it: the print's small share is counted as taken back too). The energy that would break the ground goes to the endless ocean (or growth in the growth modes); the push still goes in, but where the ground couldn't bear it, it takes only what it can and their power supplies the rest (canon: one of the few cases where momentum is absorbed; it goes to the endless ocean). What it can take is its bearing beyond the weight standing on it (his own, plus whatever he holds or catches, or the charger landing on him in Collide), over the pulse. Sideways, a bolted foot (Bolted to the planet, with They take their own recoil off) hands the planet the whole push; a foot standing on friction, at most the ground's friction on what it bears (μ × bearing × pulse). Where real ground would just have held, the cap starts at what real ground took (the stomp's kept print, the landing's feet where real ground just bears the landing), so there is no cliff there and a harder stomp never shakes the ground less. Elsewhere the ground takes its bearing over a pulse that shortens as he pushes harder (a jump's push-off, its landing's body stop, a thrown partner's landing, Collide's take-off): a shorter, higher-pitched pulse dies out sooner and plaster tolerates it better, so a harder push there can shake the ground less, and the courtesy ceiling can then name a hazard that all-out doesn't cause. An unbounded push with a push time is capped the same way. That applies to a jump's push-off and landing, a throw (gentle or all-out, the partner toss and the game of catch too) and a catch with They take their own recoil off (with it on, their own push back never reaches the ground), a stomp (the stomping foot's own bearing, over a foot's contact time, since his weight is on the other foot), a Collide hit from above, a thrown partner's landing on his own Unbreakable footing (downward; sideways he skids it out whole, as on real ground), and instant growth. A push with no push time (comic mode at infinite strength) leaves the ground none of it. The push goes in as a pulse carrying the momentum the ground takes over the longer of the push's time and a limb's contact time (a drill stamp's 52 ms, scaled with size: an estimate; Collide's hit through the holder's legs too, so the default sprint into a bolted Rex doesn't crack plaster 5 m away, though a jump onto him at a higher dial does). A jump's push-off, the foot's own push, uses its push time; instant growth, the growth time. Where the ground strains more than about 1 part in 10,000 at the nearest people's distance, the shaking figures are flagged rough (the elastic model is past its range there, as on the Walk tab). The 'break the ground' preset sets the power aside for that move (its search, effort and courtesy ceiling run on real ground); an effort the reader sets past it by hand keeps the power on (a decision: otherwise every all-out effort would break the ground, and the courtesy ceiling, worked out with the power on, could sit past the break). Lake ice and a steel deck have no ground-wave model, so their shaking isn't given. With Unbreakable footing on, instant growth can't drive his soles into the ground, so he grows upward from them, as he did on Comic ground (a question for the owners; canon has him grow about his middle); the ground's push that starts his rise (his mass times his soles' speed, over the growth time) still goes into it and shakes it (rough).
- They take their own recoil covers his throw, punch, stomp (not Collide's stomp-launch, where the stomp is his charge), a pull (only the rope's pull back: his steps are the move; his legs still move his own body, with at most the strength he uses, as in a sprint, bolted too, and on friction they need friction to do it), a shove, the thrower in Catch and Wrestle, the partner toss (the toss and the catch), the catcher's stop in the game of catch (canon, 14 Oct 2026: a catch is absorbed unless he chooses otherwise; he never slides and is never carried off), and the winner's swing in an arm-wrestling pin. Each push back goes to their source, the endless ocean, not the planet: none of it reaches the ground, and it carries almost no energy, since they don't move. With Bolted to the planet on too, their own push back still goes to the endless ocean (the power decides for their own moves). A stomp's foot blow is the move, so it reaches the ground whole and nothing cancels it (with the power off, bolted or standing, the bolts' upward pull or his standing foot's unloading, by the part of the push that doesn't lift him, partly cancels it, so that shaking is an upper bound). With the winner's power on and Unbreakable footing, a pin's slam reaches the ground whole at any swing time; with it off, a quick pin (its swing shorter than a limb's contact time) leaves the ground no net push, like a punch: the swing's push and the slam's cancel there. It doesn't yet cover the presses in arm wrestling, the push match or the Collide test (pull request 4 of BRIEF-canon.md), where Bolted to the planet is the setting for being pushed by someone else. A jump or a sprint start is the move itself, so it is unchanged. The fireball's push on a catcher (a throw that reaches him as plasma) is Never knocked back's case, also pull request 4: it still carries him off, or goes into the planet through his bolts.
- A pull has no leg-speed limit and no air drag on him (the Sprint tab has both: his legs cap his top speed, 61 m/s at the default dial, and the air's drag grows with his speed). Where his own body sets a pull's pace, his legs push as in a bolted sprint, so a towed load speeds up no faster than he sprints, but over 10 m he can end up quicker: a car pulled all-out, bolted, at the default dial takes 0.26 s and he ends at 77 m/s, where his unloaded bolted sprint takes 0.27 s. So the pull's times for light loads pulled hard are a little short, and his steps' push on the ground (his body's momentum over the pull), with the shaking it gives, a little high.
- Growth: the gradual-growth limits (100 Pa, 0.25 m/s) are borrowed from blasting and robot safety, judged on a bystander 2 m away (our choice); the instant-growth timescale and the blast range are estimates; instant growth happens about his middle (canon): on real ground his soles are driven half the height gained into it at constant speed, and the ground takes that work (its bearing plus the soil pushed aside, rough, often far more than the air blast); with Unbreakable footing on he grows upward instead. The lift the ground's push would give him goes to the endless ocean (canon, 12 Oct 2026), one of the canon's cases where momentum is absorbed; the page shows how high it would have thrown him. On a thin layer (asphalt, a slab, ice, a steel deck) his soles punch through, and the energy below uses the layer's own strength all the way down (rough). The air blast counts the downward share of the growth as air too (an upper bound). The runaway loop is a round trip (canon: side effects paid from the energy absorbed, gravity's release to the endless ocean), so it can never run away and the page has no half-life control (removed 12 Oct 2026); its gravity share is reported with the weak-field formula. Gradual growth no longer has a lift limit (it never set the time for growth the page shows; it could only below about 5 mm, which the page calls unnoticeable).
- Collide's bounce with the one hit not held, with Unbreakable footing on, books the splat's footing share in the ground while its after-speeds ignore footing: about 1.1% too much momentum in the ground. The momentum ledger leaves that case out of its sum and holds it under 2%; Collide is pull request 4 of `BRIEF-canon.md`.
- Walking: a foot that would sink past half his hip height in clay counts as mired (he can't walk there); Ollie's boots on soft clay do. A heavy landing past the clay's bearing drives the foot in like a small stomp (Poncelet penetration, an upper bound).

- Footing on the page is binary: just standing (friction), or Bolted to the planet. The engine still has a third stance, claws or boots dug in (`'braced'`), which `test/calibration.js`, `test/presence.js` and the all-out baseline use; the page no longer offers it (at their strength the ground tears apart around the claws, so it mostly described the ground failing), and a saved dug-in setting loads as just standing. The engine reads bolting move by move, and the page says what it does on each tab. A push along the ground can't slide a planted bolted foot. Walking and jumping ignore it. On weak ground a bolted body still sinks under its own weight and when it lifts, throws or lands, with two exceptions: on Sprint a bolted stride neither slips nor sinks (the engine drops both limits there), and in Collide a bolted body landed on from above is held up by his bolts. In the shoving match a bolted wrestler stays planted: he holds, but doesn't walk the other out. A bolted partner can't be lifted. In Collide a bolted pusher's feet hold without limit. A bolted charger gets his speed from a bolted sprint and can't stomp-launch at all, but if he bounces off he slides back on friction alone (once the hit is over, the engine bolts only the one who was hit). The ground around bolted feet is checked against ground shear strength × a buried area (both estimates, Sources table), unless Unbreakable footing is on, only in Arm wrestle once the table breaks, Wrestle's push when both are bolted, the shove at unlimited strength into a bolted partner (around both sets of bolts when both are bolted and the shover's recoil isn't taken; with it taken, around the partner's only), and Collide for a bolted body being hit or pushed (and an unlimited bolted pusher against a truly immovable one); on real ground (not with Unbreakable footing on) a Collide hit too fast for a stress wave to cross the rock is over before the bolts can help. Elsewhere (the solo tabs, Catch, a single bolted wrestler, a finite shove, a bolted pusher in Collide) the model doesn't check it, however hard the push. `BRIEF-gui.md` section 3 asked to drop the Sources row 'Claw shear strength used when dug in' as unused; it is kept, renamed 'Ground shear strength (where the ground tears out around bolted feet)', because the bolted tear-out (engine `groundTear`, Collide's `tearCap`) and the clay footprints and sinking (`clayDepth`, on the Walk and Weight tabs) still use it; its label now names both.
- The Rex + Ollie element works nothing out while it is closed. Open, a settings change redraws Rex's bay first and the element after the next paint, and while the strength dial is being dragged the element waits for the drag to stop, so it can lag Rex's bay by a moment.

- Real-muscle speeds follow the Hill curve with a single-muscle shape factor. Whole limbs are flatter, so real-muscle results are a model, not a measurement.
- The body mass is set by ruling (3,000 kg each, 300 kg of it Rex's tail; the picture alone gave the tail about 307 kg). The picture measured about 11–12% less body than the ruled mass needs at 1,060 kg/m³; side views will show whether the bodies are that much deeper. Dinosaur-style air sacs could make Rex about 7% lighter at the same size.
- Ground is a bearing-capacity-plus-inertia model. It does not model ice sheets snapping in bending, layered soils, or ejecta.
- Energy comparisons: the ladder mixes kinds of energy (blasts, motion, food, electricity, sunlight, mass turned to energy) on one scale, so a comparison shows size, not a like-for-like match (owner's decision, 12 Oct 2026: one shared ladder). Several rungs are typical cases rather than one event: a Corolla and a loaded semi-truck at 100 km/h (chosen speeds), a day's and a month's food at 2,000 Calories, a day of an average American home's electricity, an average hurricane's day (an illustrative NOAA figure), all the light of a supernova, a supernova's blast, and the total energy of a massive star's supernova. Ten rungs are rough (uncertain by more than 2×, with a published spread that wide, or, for an average hurricane, an illustrative figure the lab judges rough): a lightning bolt, Beirut, Krakatoa, an average hurricane's heat, the dinosaur-killing asteroid, a nova, the 2004 magnetar flare, all the light of a supernova, a supernova's blast and SN 1998bw; between them and their neighbours a figure is only as good as the rough rung. Where a rough rung sets another number, the page says so: the growth what-if gives the Krakatoa and dinosaur-killer energies and the mass they make to one figure, and the height and density they change to two, then says every figure after that is rough too; the strength dial's 'Punch out-hits the dinosaur-killer impact' milestone is given as about one figure, with a note that it could sit up to about 30× higher. Statuses short of verified or derived: the MOAB's 11 t of TNT is reported (no official US figure; it agrees with its fill), Pluto's binding energy is an estimate (its ice density is assumed), and six rungs are disputed (a lightning bolt, Beirut, Krakatoa, the dinosaur-killer, the 2004 magnetar flare and SN 1998bw). The widest steps are 70× (a year of sunlight to the Sun's output for a second) and 33× (Hiroshima to Chelyabinsk; a day of sunlight to the dinosaur-killer), so energies there get shares down to about 12% or multiples up to about 8×.
- Crater sizes past a few hundred km and anything near Earth's binding energy are rough or flagged as planet-scale.
- Flight counts only Earth's gravity; arcs past the Moon's orbit are flagged as rough.
- In the game of catch, the arm stroke has a set time only when it is forced (he slides or needs at least half his strength). Otherwise he can bring the catch in as slowly as he likes, and the round trip counts only the throws and flights.

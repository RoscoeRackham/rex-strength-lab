## Contests and the two Never knocked back switches (BRIEF-canon.md pull request 4)

Canon (CLAUDE.md, the rulings of 14 Oct 2026): "*Never knocked back* is a default power for each of them, with a switch each, both on by default. … In contests, force moves, so Never knocked back is set aside. Contests are arm wrestling, every Wrestle move, Collide's charge and steady push, and the partner toss. In a contest the other's push is real, and only the push each puts on himself is absorbed (his own recoil). A charger's push back from his own hit is his own recoil: it goes to his source, so he doesn't slow. Where the one he hits is held without limit (never knocked back, or held up by their powers), the collision has no physical answer." This pull request builds item 4 of `BRIEF-canon.md`. It is stacked on pull requests 2 and 3. The engine is commit `0f27012`; the page, the cards and the tests follow it. Its review rounds are next.

**What changed.** With They take their own recoil on (the default), every two-player test is a contest that lets force move: the other's push on him is real, and only the push back of his own move goes to his source, the endless ocean.
- Collide's charge: the charger doesn't slow. The one he hits is carried along at his speed (a splat) or sent off at twice it (a perfect bounce, by relativistic velocity addition near light speed), and the charger's source supplies the momentum. The hit's freed energy is taken back by default and is real only with a blast preset. Where their powers hold the one hit without limit (bolted on ground Unbreakable footing holds, or landed on from above with it holding him up), the collision has no physical answer, and the tab explains that first. Bolted on real rock, the rock around his bolts tears out and he is carried with a plug of rock.
- Collide's steady push: the pusher's own footing doesn't cap his push. The force between their hands is still the same both ways; only the pusher's side of it goes to his source instead of through his feet.
- Wrestle: in the shoving match each pushes with the effort he uses, and only his feet hold him against the other's push, so two equal bodies are shoved apart. In Both at once each is shoved by the other's shove. The Lift tab says its partner doesn't resist.
- Arm wrestling: each elbow pad holds the other's press (reading B, below). A pin never breaks the table with the power on; standing, a press isn't capped by his own footing.
- Never knocked back: two switches in Their powers, "Never knocked back: Rex" and "Never knocked back: Ollie", on by default. The one case the lab has is a throw that reaches the catcher as a fireball or plasma in the game of catch: with his switch on he isn't moved and its momentum goes to the endless ocean.
With They take their own recoil off every contest is as before (pull request 3's page), and with every power off every engine result is unchanged (`test/powers.js` part 1).

**Decisions** (the review rounds check them):
1. The charger of unbounded mass. With the charger's own recoil taken he doesn't slow, so in his frame the one hit arrives at his speed v and meets an immovable body: the immovable sums apply with the roles swapped (the brief's "after Q13" sums). The one hit leaves at v (splat) or at 2v/(1 + v²/c²) (bounce), with momentum γmv or 2γ²mv. The charger's source supplies that gain plus what the one hit's footing hands the ground during the hit (its hold, at most the hit's force, × the hit's time; none in the bounce bound, whose speeds ignore footing). At light speed the old answer stands: the one hit is knocked away at just under light speed.
2. How long he is carried. A charger who never slows could carry the one hit as long as he likes. The lab ends the carry at the end of the squash: the charger stops himself there (his own push, so its push back goes to his source too), and the one hit slides on from the charger's speed (or, from above on real ground, is driven in). This is a choice, stated as a limit.
3. The paradox. Held without limit by their powers means bolted on ground that Unbreakable footing holds (or the ground the lab treats as holding when they would sink), from any side, or landed on from above where that ground holds him up. The charger can't slow and the one hit can't move: canon rules that this has no physical answer. The tab says so first, in its verdict, before any chip, tile or the effort box: the charger takes his own recoil, so nothing can slow him; the one hit is held without limit by their powers; one of them has to give and neither can. It works out nothing of the hit. Bolted on real rock is not held without limit: the rock gives.
4. The Truly immovable thought experiment stays as it was: it sets the charger's own recoil aside (it is about the test, not their powers), and its verdict says so in one sentence.
5. The hit's energy. Canon: "A hit's freed energy is absorbed by default (it shows in his shimmer) and is real only when he means it." The lab reads "when he means it" as a blast preset (rattle the windows, break windows, flatten houses, a block), the way "break the ground" sets Unbreakable footing aside (the brief's reading). With the charger's power on and such a preset, the effort search and the tab run a "mean" variant in which the splat's energy is real; otherwise it is taken back, and the page says the shimmer that shows it isn't drawn yet. The courtesy ceiling stays the default run's. The one hit's own air past 1% of light speed and the charger's run-up air are their bodies meeting real air, so they are always real.
6. From above on real ground (Unbreakable footing off, as the break-the-ground preset sets it), the one hit is driven into the ground at the charger's speed by the lab's landing model: any landing now breaks hard ground.
7. The steady push. The pusher's own footing doesn't cap his push (his push back goes to his source), and he never slips. The one pushed holds with his footing, or his bolts and the rock around them; only his side's rock is loaded. Unlimited against a hold without limit is the one-sided paradox; against bolts on real rock only his rock tears. The tile "Force between them" now gives the push between their hands: driven back, that is the pusher's whole push, of which the feet of the one driven back pass only their hold to the ground.
8. The shoving match. No push costs the pusher his grip: each pushes with the effort he uses, and each is held only by his footing against the other's push. Both pushes beat the other's grip: both are shoved apart. Each is pushed by the other's push, less his own hold, over the shove's stroke (2.44 m, an estimate), then slides on his feet (real muscle: an upper bound). Only one beats: the winner walks the loser out at the Pull tab's pace for his own body, a = min((his push − the loser's hold) ÷ the loser's mass, (his footing − the loser's push) ÷ his own mass), or, bolted (or unable to walk), shoves him off. Even the least effort the two-player search uses (enough to stand and walk, 2,570 kg-force each at 3,000 kg) is more than either one's grip on any ground here (μ is at most 0.84, on gravel), so two equal bodies are always shoved apart with the power on: there is no dead heat between them. The Wrestle tab says so.
9. Both at once: each is shoved by the other's shove, worked out as the Shove tab works it out, so equal bodies both fly apart (an upper bound: both moving at once, each push runs out of stroke sooner).
10. Arm wrestling: reading B, not the brief's A. Under A each one's push back would go to his source, so the presses would no longer load the table at all. But the other's press on him is real, and his forearm, pivoted on his elbow pad, can hold against it only by pushing back on the pad (or by moving). With nothing on his side carrying it, a real force would act on each hand in a stalemate while nothing moved: momentum would vanish outside canon's list (Never knocked back is set aside in contests). So the lab builds B, the shoving match's rule at the table: each pad, or standing each one's feet, holds the other's press; only what is truly his own push back goes to his source. On the table a stalemate loads each pad as before, and both presses past what the table carries break it, as before. In a pin the winner's pad holds the loser's press; spinning both forearms is the winner's swing, whose push back goes to his source, so a pin never breaks the table with the power on. Standing (only with Things keep their form off), his own footing no longer caps his own press, so the stronger press always pins, and each one's feet hold against the other's press: they slide where it beats them. Both presses unlimited, standing: no physical answer where either is held without limit, the rock tears around both sets of bolts on real rock, and otherwise they are flung apart. Under A the table would never break with the power on (0 of 2,160 runs), so A's standing case could never arise.
11. Lift and throw a partner: the thrower's push back goes to his source (pull request 2). Holding the partner up is weight, not recoil, so it stays on the lifter's feet. The Lift tab's "can't make himself heavier by pushing down … that push stays between the two of them" holds only with the power off; with it on the tab says the lift models a partner who lets himself be lifted, since a resisting partner's push down would be real.
12. The switches. New state keys `noKnockback` (Rex) and `oNoKnockback` (Ollie), true by default; an old save has neither, so it loads with both on (no save mark needed). Each has a validator; neither is in the engine's `anyPower` or the page's `anyPowerOn` (both default to on, so counting them would change every run with the other powers off). Two switches sit next to the Bolted pair, each named for a screen reader and both described by one note: on, a hit from outside a contest doesn't move him and its momentum goes to the endless ocean, even if he is bolted; in their contests (every two-player test here) they let force move, and a catch is his own push, taken back through They take their own recoil; so the switch acts in one case only, a throw that reaches the catcher as a fireball or plasma in the game of catch. The summary line lists them with the other powers ("never knocked back", or one name when only one is on), and the Their powers card says the same in one sentence. Each one's context carries his own switch.
13. The fireball's push. With the catcher's switch on he isn't moved, bolted or not (the power wins over Bolted, as They take their own recoil does), and its momentum goes to the endless ocean. Of the energy it frees where he stands, the heat made in the air on the way is real; its motion as it stops at him is taken back unless he means it (a blast preset). With the switch off, as before: he and the lump move off together, or, bolted, the Earth takes it. The fireball needs Things keep their form off and some of the air's drag left, so with the default powers neither switch changes anything.
14. Rex's switch shows. The return leg of the game of catch was worked out only when the bodies differ; now also when the two switches differ, and where both legs reach the catcher as a fireball the verdict gives the return leg's push on Rex.
15. The test of the switches (`test/shots.js`): on by default, each named, next to Bolted, with its note; an old save loads with both on; turning either off changes no result on any tab at its defaults; the fireball case shows each one.

The two-player effort search counts both sliding in the shoving match and Both at once, and the one hit carried at the charger's speed in Collide (its slide once the charger stops); the Collide hit's blast, and a fireball's motion freed at a catcher with Never knocked back on, count only for a blast preset; the rock tearing under a carried, bolted holder counts as the ground breaking; the shoving match's reason for not judging the shaking states the power-on case.

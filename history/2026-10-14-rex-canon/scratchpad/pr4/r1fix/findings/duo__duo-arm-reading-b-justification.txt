### reasoning
Not a defect. Decision 10 is a recorded choice with a sound reason, and it is flagged to the owner in 'What is left' ('arm-wrestling reading B (decision 10)'). The duo's version of A works by letting each one's source take his own arm's reaction at the elbow and shoulder. In a stalemate that reaction equals the other's press, so his source in effect holds him against the other's real press. That is Never knocked back, which canon sets aside in contests ('In a contest the other's push is real'). Under that model the other's press never loads or moves anything but the clasped hands. The lab's contest model, the one the brief itself uses for the shoving match, treats each push as acting on the other and sends only its own push back to the source. In that model, if the pad doesn't carry the other's real press, nothing does, and momentum vanishes in a stalemate. So the README's argument holds within the model the lab uses. Q15 makes the shoving match follow 'the arm-wrestle rule'. If the duo's reading A were applied there, equal pushers would deadlock with nothing loading their feet, contradicting the brief's own shoving-match row ('two equal pushers both get shoved apart'). B is the consistent reading of both. B also does keep the own push backs in the ledger: they go to the shared source as +P·t and −P·t, which cancel, so no shown result differs. That is not 'absorbs nothing' in any visible sense. The consequences the duo lists (the table breaks at 6.1 t; standing, both pushed off their feet) are stated in 'What moved' and decision 10, where the owner will see them. At most the engine comment's 'physically incoherent' could say 'incoherent with the lab's contest rule (the shoving match)', but that is wording, not a defect.

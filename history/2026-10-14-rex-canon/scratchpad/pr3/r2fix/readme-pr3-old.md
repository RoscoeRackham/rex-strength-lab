## Safe passenger on by default (BRIEF-canon.md pull request 3)

Canon (CLAUDE.md, "Default powers", on unless they choose otherwise): "*Safe passenger* (RT 4.2): someone he throws or catches feels a manageable force unless he knows they can take it." And the ruling of 14 Oct 2026: "*Safe passenger* also covers where the person lands and the air's drag on them." Until this pull request the switch was off by default and covered only his push, at the launch and in a catch; the air's drag in flight and the landing stayed real. This pull request builds item 5 of `BRIEF-canon.md`. It is stacked on pull request 2.

**What changed.** Safe passenger is on by default. In Their powers it has its own heading, "People they throw or catch", and the intro and the summary line count it among the default powers. With it on, a person he throws or catches feels only a manageable push, spread through their whole body, at the launch, in the air and when they land. So the passenger hazard (the ride limits) never fires, on Throw or in the game of catch. They come down unharmed: the landing's energy is taken back, but its push still reaches the ground, so it shakes the ground as a real landing does. That shaking now sets the person throw's courtesy ceiling. With the switch off, everything is as before, and the page says the lab doesn't judge the landing's fall. Only the person is a passenger; Ollie never is.

**Decisions** (the review rounds check them):
1. On by default: `DEFAULTS.safePassenger` is true.
2. The save mark goes up to 3. Every save stored `safePassenger`, which was false by default, so the key alone can't tell an old default from a choice. A save with no mark, or marked 2, loads with Safe passenger on; a save marked 3 keeps the reader's choice. Why 3 and not pull request 1's 2: `STATUS.md` asks the owner to republish after #31, and a save written on that page carries the mark 2 with Safe passenger off as the old default. Reading it as a choice would leave the new default off for that visitor. The 3,007 kg migration still applies only below the mark 2, so a save marked 2 keeps a visitor's own 3,007 kg. Every save is now marked 3.
3. The engine splits the passenger rule in two: `passengerLaunch` (the launch past the ride limits) and `passengerDrag` (the drag in flight past the held 1.5 g). The hazard is `passenger`: Safe passenger off and either of them. With the switch off that is exactly the old rule; with it on it never fires. A new `passengerLanding` gives the landing taken back: `absorbed`, the impact energy in J, and `J`, its momentum in kg·m/s. It gives them only with the switch on, for a person who lands below 1% of light speed, and otherwise null. It is a function beside the hazards, not a key of the throw's result, so no result changes shape. Safe passenger stays out of `anyPower`, so it never turns on the count of his hand's own air.
4. The landing's split (the brief's reading; for review to confirm): the person comes down unharmed; the landing's momentum still reaches the ground; its energy is taken back, as Soft landing does for his own landings (pull request 5 will show it as shimmer). The ground's pulse is the person's own stop, about their size over the landing speed, as for any landing: 31 ms at the least disruptive effort, about 59 g on average. Safe passenger spreads that push through them; it doesn't make a slow, gentle stop. Taking back the energy doesn't take back the force (as with Soft landing for his own feet), and the momentum stays real and whole (no new canon). So past what the ground bears, even with the push spread over the person's whole facing area (0.67 m², less their weight; on sand and gravel the bearing grows with that patch's width, taken as its square root), the ground gives way where they land, and the page says so instead of 'no crater'; the lab doesn't size the hole (review round 1, `PASSENGER.landGround`). That starts at a landing of about 100 m/s on the default concrete slab (102 m/s; the same on asphalt), 140 m/s on ice, 5.6 km/s on steel, 13 km/s on granite, and 20–50 m/s on sand, gravel and clay (estimates: a generous contact patch). Past it the shaking figures, from the elastic ground model, are rough. At the default dial the person throw stays below it even all-out (81 m/s); thrown all-out at 45° with a dial of about 145,000 kg, it reaches it. (Read as a gentle 1.5 g stop instead, the pulse would last 1.2 s and the shaking 5 m away would fall from 2.5 to 0.0013 mm/s. The landing would never crack plaster, so the person throw would have no courtesy ceiling at the default dial, and at a 10⁹ kg dial the sonic boom, at about 1.6 million kg-force, would set it.)
5. No landing blast for a person with the switch on, since its energy is taken back: the landing part of the throw's blast is 0 for them. A very fast person's impact line says the energy is taken back, so no blast. It doesn't say 'no quake': the landing's push still shakes the ground, and the effort box gives how far (review round 1). It says 'no crater' only where the ground bears the push (decision 4); past that it says the ground gives way. Past 1% of light speed nothing changes on the Throw tab: there the "landing" part is the fireball in the air, not a landing (with all of the drag absorbed such a throw meets no air and leaves the Earth). For the game of catch's throw that hits the ground with no air, see decision 7.
6. Heat isn't covered. Safe passenger covers the push at the launch, the drag in flight and the landing, not heating. Where Things keep their form is off and the page says the person would melt or burn away (or that the lab doesn't model whether they do), it doesn't also call them unharmed. The game of catch follows the Throw tab's rule (Things keep their form off, some of the air's drag left, and the air at the nose past 1,200 K): the missed person's callout and the palm tile don't call them unharmed, and the Game of catch card says Safe passenger doesn't cover heating (review round 1).
7. The game of catch: Safe passenger covers the throw, the flight and the catch (`catchPassengerG` checks the switch first, so the drag no longer counts either). If the catch is missed, the person comes down unharmed: the callout says the landing's energy is taken back and its push still shakes the ground (past what the ground bears, decision 4, the ground gives way where they land; where the flight heats them, decision 6). With the switch on the first hit of a catch squashes only his palms, not the person. A throw that hits the ground (past 1% of light speed, more than about 12 km apart) is no catch. With Air resistance absorbed at 100% it meets no air on the way, so for a person it is their landing, and Safe passenger covers it as it does any landing (the ruling: it covers where they land; review round 1): its energy is taken back, so no crater, quake or blast counts as harm (no blast block, and the effort box's blast leaves it out); its push still reaches the ground, and at that speed the push is far past what the ground bears, so the ground gives way where they land. Below 100% the air it meets turns to plasma and rides with it into the ground, so it stays a failed throw with its crater, quake and blast, as before.
8. Where the switch sits: Their powers has no "default powers" heading, so Safe passenger gets its own, "People they throw or catch", between Things keep their form and Air resistance absorbed. The summary line reads "default powers on" only when all five are on, and lists Safe passenger with the others when one of them is off.
9. The copy: the switch, the Throw tab's chip, text and "Lands at" tile, the effort box and the cards say the person feels a manageable push, spread through them, at the launch, in the air and when they land; that the landing's push still reaches the ground and shakes it; and that its energy is taken back. With the switch off, the texts are as before, and the landing stays a fall the lab doesn't judge. The effort box credits Safe passenger where the same throw, all-out with it off, presses the person past the ride limits ("Safe passenger is on, so it leaves out the person pressed past the ride limits").
10. Research data: `comic_passenger_drag_g` (an estimate) said the drag was "not removed by safe passenger once released". Its label and note now say the figures judge the drag with Safe passenger off; its status is unchanged.
11. The powers-off fixture: 8 of its planner cases run with Safe passenger on (a person thrown at unlimited strength, with the second switch set in `test/powers-off-cases.js`), so they aren't powers-off cases. Their passenger hazard (on `main`, the drag) is now never. `test/powers.js` part 1 compares them through a test-side shim that restores the old rule, and checks that without the shim exactly those 8 differ. The fixture isn't regenerated.

**What moved** (against pull request 2's branch; the 3,000 kg Rex, a person thrown 30 m at 45°, people 5 m away, the page's default dial unless stated):
- At the defaults, least disruptive goes from 333 kg-force (0.37% of his strength; the person goes 3.0 m, held below the ride limits) to 3,342 kg-force (3.7%). Released at 16.4 m/s, the person goes 30 m and lands at 18.0 m/s, like a 16.5 m fall; 14,600 J is taken back. The courtesy ceiling goes from 334 kg-force (the person's g-force) to about 27,000 kg-force (30%), where plaster may crack 5 m from the landing. All-out (80.8 m/s, landing 668 m away) the launch no longer counts as harm. This move comes from the new default alone: at 100% Air resistance absorbed there is no drag, so with the switch on both branches give the same figures.
- Below 100% Air resistance absorbed, with the switch on, the drag no longer counts. At 50% the ceiling was about 78,200 kg-force (the drag); now there is none. At 0%, with the other three powers on or off, it was about 39,300–39,600 kg-force; now there is none. With all four powers off, all-out, the person lands 214 m away at 34.5 m/s, and plaster may crack only within 1.4 m of the landing.
- The landing blast: with the switch on, a person landing past the speed of sound makes no blast. At a 10⁹ kg dial (the four powers on) the courtesy ceiling stays at about 27,000 kg-force (plaster), and the sonic boom and the air blast from his throw still start at about 1.6 million kg-force, but the windows preset moves from about 1.8 million to 388 million kg-force and the houses preset from about 1.6 million to 241 million.
- The game of catch with a person: by default no ride-limit harm. At the default dial nothing in the gentle lob harms anyone, so they go all-out. With the switch off, as before, even the lob at the least they need to stand and walk is past the ride limits (2.3 g, against the 1.5 g rides allow for that long), so nothing is harmless there. With it on, a fastball in real air (Air resistance absorbed at 0%) used to be flagged for its drag (3.5 g); now it isn't.

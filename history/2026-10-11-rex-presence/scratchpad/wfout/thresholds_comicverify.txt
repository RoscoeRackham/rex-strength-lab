SUMMARY: Research topic "thresholds" (vibration levels at which people feel it, things rattle, cosmetic damage starts, and people lose their footing), for the Walk tab. 46 facts. I fetched and read every primary table myself, apart from the paywalled standards:
- Caltrans (2020): Tables 4-7, 13, 19-22, plus its appended 2004 Technical Advisory reproducing Whiffin & Leonard (1971) LR 418.
- FTA (2018): Report 0123, via the ROSA P copy, because transit.dot.gov returned 403.
- BS 5228-2:2009 Annex B: a licensed copy posted by a county council. It reproduces BS 7385-2 Table 1. The A1:2014 footnotes come from an An Bord Pleanala appendix.
- DIN 4150-3: the 1999 table via Watercare (Auckland); the 2016 table via An Bord Pleanala.
- USBM RI 8507: scanned, read by OCR and page images. Covers safe levels, the Figure B-1 curve, the Table 15 duration table, and the wall-amplification and rattling statements.
- Earthquake scales: EMS-98, the USGS abridged MMI and the JMA intensity table.
- Wald et al. (1999) PGV/PGA by intensity, as reproduced in the USGS ShakeMap manual (TM 12-A1). I re-derived the band edges from its equations.
- Takahashi et al. (2004): a shake-table test of when standing people lose balance.

Main findings:
- **(a) People feel it:** about 0.14-0.3 mm/s PPV for people indoors (BS 5228-2; Whiffin; ISO 2631:1989 via Caltrans; FTA 65 VdB ≈ 0.2 mm/s PPV). A single isolated transient needs more: Caltrans gives 1.0 mm/s, and USBM Table 15 shows the average person needs 2.4-3.3 mm/s for a 0.5-1 s event.
- **(b) Rattling:** no standard gives a number. Caltrans and FTA say dishes and windows can rattle at or below perception. Earthquake scales put rattling at intensity IV (PGA 1.4-3.9 %g). Converted by acceleration, that is about 1-3 mm/s at 20 Hz; this is my derivation.
- **(c) Cosmetic damage:**
  - BS 7385-2, homes: 15 mm/s at 4 Hz, 20 mm/s at 15 Hz, 50 mm/s from 40 Hz. Probability of damage "tends towards zero at 12.5 mm/s". Minor damage above 2x these values, major above 4x; up to 50% lower for continuous vibration.
  - USBM: 12.7 mm/s plaster, 19 mm/s drywall below 40 Hz, 51 mm/s above 40 Hz.
  - Caltrans, older homes: 12.7 mm/s transient, 7.6 mm/s continuous.
  - DIN 4150-3, dwellings: 5 mm/s below 10 Hz.
  - FTA, non-engineered timber: 5.1 mm/s.
- **(d) Footing:** no standard covers this. Earthquake intensities VI-VIII ("a few lose balance" to "difficult to stand even outdoors") match PGV 81-600 mm/s. On the shake table, standing people first lost balance at about 0.3 m/s at 0.3-2.5 Hz, and nobody did at 5 Hz up to 0.48 m/s.

Recommended page values:
- feel 0.3 mm/s (range 0.14-1)
- rattle about 1 mm/s (range 0.3-3; estimate)
- cosmetic damage 12.7 mm/s for one step (7.6 mm/s for a walk past; range 5-50)
- losing footing about 300 mm/s (range 80-600; estimate from the earthquake analogy, probably cautious for footsteps)

I did not rely on any existing final-*.json key; none covers ground-vibration thresholds. The existing blast_window_breakage key is air overpressure, not ground vibration.

MODEL: 1) THRESHOLDS FOR VIB.TH (m/s, compared with the engine's free-field ground PPV from ppvAt):
- feel = 0.3e-3 (keep the placeholder). Set by BS 5228-2 Table B.1, "might be just perceptible in residential environments", for construction vibration (about 8-80 Hz) felt indoors.
  - Show the range 0.14e-3 (most sensitive, BS 5228-2) to 1.0e-3 (Caltrans Table 20: barely perceptible for a single isolated transient).
  - Use 0.3e-3 for a walk past, which is a train of steps.
  - For one isolated step, the page may say "the most sensitive notice from about 0.3 mm/s; most people from about 1 mm/s".
- rattle = 1.0e-3 (keep the placeholder), but label it an ESTIMATE; no standard gives it.
  - Better, if the engine has a dominant step frequency f (from the attenuation/elephant research): v_rattle = max(0.3e-3, 0.137 / (2 pi f)). Here 0.137 m/s^2 is the bottom of intensity IV, "china, glasses, windows and doors rattle", per the Wald 1999 acceleration band.
  - Show the range 0.3e-3 to 3e-3.
- plaster (call it "cosmetic damage" on the page) = 12.7e-3 for a single step or stomp, i.e. transient (USBM RI 8507 plaster limit below 40 Hz; Caltrans older homes, transient; BS 7385-2 "tends towards zero at 12.5 mm/s").
  - For a walk past (repeated steps, like an impact pile driver), use 7.6e-3 (Caltrans older homes, continuous/frequent intermittent).
  - The current placeholder of 5e-3 equals the strictest sources (DIN 4150-3 dwellings below 10 Hz; FTA timber; Whiffin & Leonard continuous). It is defensible as a "fragile building" option, but not as the default.
  - Optional frequency curve, BS 7385-2 line 2 (homes, transient). The interpolation is my assumption: straight lines on the standard's log-log Figure B.1.
    - f < 4 Hz: v = 2 pi f x 0.6e-3
    - 4-15 Hz: v = 15e-3 x (f/4)^0.2177
    - 15-40 Hz: v = 20e-3 x (f/15)^0.934
    - f >= 40 Hz: v = 50e-3
    - Halve it for continuous vibration if the walk is treated as continuous.
- footing = 0.3 (currently 0.2), labelled an ESTIMATE from earthquakes. It is where EMS-98 VIII ("Many people find it difficult to stand, even outdoors") starts on Wald's PGV scale (310 mm/s), and where Takahashi 2004's standing subjects first lost balance (about 0.26-0.31 m/s at 0.3-2.5 Hz). Range 0.08 (EMS VI, "a few persons lose their balance", indoors) to 0.6. Say it probably overstates the risk for footsteps.
- Optional extra marker (derived physics, not a standard): loose objects hop when the vertical acceleration exceeds 1 g, i.e. v = 9.80665 / (2 pi f) (78 mm/s at 20 Hz).

2) NOT-SO-HEROIC PRESETS (property damage):
- "rattle the windows" = rattle threshold.
- "crack the plaster" = cosmetic (12.7 mm/s, or the BS 7385-2 curve).
- "real cracks" = 2x the BS 7385-2 value (minor damage: loose plaster, hairline-to-3 mm cracks; about 30-100 mm/s depending on frequency).
- "structural damage" = 4x the BS 7385-2 value (major damage).
- Cross-check against Siskind's 50% probabilities: cosmetic 64 mm/s, minor 127, major 152 (blasting, homes). These presets are thresholds; the reach distance comes from ppvReach.

3) WHAT EACH THRESHOLD IGNORES (put on the page):
- All of them are PPV at the ground or the building base, as the largest single component. The damage criteria use that point by definition. The human criteria were set for people inside buildings, where floors and walls can amplify (USBM: about 1.5x for the whole house at 4-12 Hz, about 4x for midwalls at 10-25 Hz, below 1x above 40 Hz). For an indoor listener, rattling and feeling start at lower ground PPV. FTA: ground vibration is "almost never a problem outdoors".
- The damage criteria come from blasting (1-2 s events, 4-40+ Hz) and construction. A single step fits Caltrans's "transient" class (like a "drop ball"); a walk fits "continuous/frequent intermittent" (like an impact pile driver). That mapping is our reading.
- The earthquake-based numbers (rattling, footing) come from shaking at about 1-6 Hz lasting many seconds with centimetre displacements. A footstep is one short, higher-frequency pulse with sub-millimetre displacement. People respond to acceleration below about 8 Hz, so velocity thresholds do not transfer directly. That is why rattling is converted by acceleration, and why the footing value is called cautious.
- Short events are harder to notice (USBM Table 15: the mean barely-perceptible level is 0.51 mm/s for 300 s, 2.4 mm/s for 1 s, and 3.3 mm/s for 0.5 s). The page uses steady-vibration values, so it may say people notice a step sooner than they would.

- Feel: "0.3 mm/s, BS 5228-2, for people indoors near construction; outdoors, and for one short step, people notice less."

- 0.5 in/s = 12.7 mm/s
- 65 VdB = 0.045 mm/s rms (PPV about 0.18 mm/s with crest factor 4)
- intensity IV PGA 0.137 m/s^2 at 20 Hz = 1.09 mm/s
- BS 7385-2 line 2 at 4/15/40 Hz = 15/20/50 mm/s

GAPS: What I could not find or verify, and what the page should say instead:
- **Rattling:** I found no standard, agency guide or measurement giving a ground-vibration PPV at which windows, crockery or ornaments rattle. Caltrans, FTA and USBM say only that rattling happens at or below the level people feel. The number offered here (about 1 mm/s, 0.3-3) is my conversion of earthquake intensity IV acceleration to footstep frequencies. The page must label it an estimate and say no standard sets it.
- **Losing footing:** no standard covers footing loss from ground vibration, and nothing covers short, high-frequency pulses. The values come from earthquake intensity descriptions (EMS-98, JMA, which give no numbers) joined to Wald 1999's California PGV bands, plus one small shake-table study (Takahashi 2004; about 30 young subjects; sinusoids; "have not find clear limit value"; values read from a graph). That EMS-98 and California MMI are about equal at VI-VIII is assumed, not verified. The page should say "estimate from earthquakes; probably cautious for footsteps".
- **Paywalled standards:** I did not open BS 7385-2, DIN 4150-3, ISO 2631-2 or BS 6472-1.
  - BS 7385-2 Table 1 comes from BS 5228-2:2009 itself, a council-posted licensed copy, which reproduces it.
  - DIN 4150-3:1999 comes from Watercare's reproduction.
  - DIN 4150-3:2016 comes from An Bord Pleanala's reproduction. Its dwellings 50-100 Hz cell reads "40 to 50", against "15 to 20" in 1999, so I marked it disputed.
  - The ISO 2631 (1989) criteria come only via Caltrans Table 7. That the 2003 edition has no limits is from a consultant's evidence (reported).
  - BS 6472-1 appears only as HS2's VDV criteria (VDV does not convert to a single PPV).
  - The BS 5228-2 A1:2014 footnotes were seen only in the Pleanala reproduction.
- **Wald 1999:** the paper is paywalled and was not opened; I used USGS's reproduction in the ShakeMap manual. Below intensity V its PGV relation was "imposed", not fitted. ShakeMap now uses Worden et al. (2012), whose ranges differ; I did not fetch it.
- **Other secondary reproductions:**
  - Siskind's damage-probability numbers (Caltrans Table 13) were not cross-checked against RI 8507's probability plots.
  - Whiffin & Leonard (LR 418), Wiss (1974), Wiss & Parmelee (1974) and Reiher & Meister (1931) were seen only as reproduced by Caltrans or USBM.
  - The FTA manual's official URL (transit.dot.gov) returned 403. I used the identical report on the US DOT ROSA P repository.
- **Footstep frequency:** the dominant frequency and duration of a footstep's ground vibration are not part of this topic; they must come from the attenuation/elephant research. Until then, the page should show the frequency-dependent values at a stated assumed frequency (for example 20 Hz) and say it is an assumption. Caltrans assumes 10-30 Hz for construction and traffic sources.
- **Indoor amplification and direction:** the frequency-specific amplification for an indoor listener (floors, walls) is sourced only as typical USBM factors (1.5 for the whole house, 4 for midwalls). The page should say that indoors people and objects respond at lower ground PPV. Damage criteria use peak component PPV; the attenuation law's component (vertical or largest) should be checked against this.

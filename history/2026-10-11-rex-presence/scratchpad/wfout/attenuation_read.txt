SUMMARY: Topic "attenuation": how hard the ground shakes (peak particle velocity, PPV) at 1–200 m from a step of a 3 t body. 49 facts: 25 verified, 15 derived, 6 estimates, 3 reported.

What I verified from the fetched documents:
- **BS 5228-2 Table E.1, dynamic compaction row.** PPV_res ≤ 0.037 (√W_h/x)^1.7 mm/s, with W_h in J. Valid for 5–100 m and 1–12 MJ. Source: a licensed copy of the standard filed as a public-inquiry core document. Table E.2's k_p values are also verified.
- **Mayne 1985 law.** v ≤ 92 (√(M·H)/x)^1.7 mm/s, as printed in TRL Report 429. It is the same law as the BS row in different units.
- **TRL 429's own warning.** The law is "less reliable for low energy" and under-predicted a 7 t weight dropped 1.2 m (82 kJ) by "up to an order of magnitude".
- **FHWA charts.** Lukas 1986 and GEC 1 (1995) plot PPV against scaled energy, with eight soil lines (both charts verified). Dropped-weight vibration is 3–10 Hz (Lukas) and 5–50 Hz at two Canadian sites (Lauzon et al.).
- **The one in-range measurement found.** Colombero et al. 2015, Pisa: 130 kg dropped about 3 m (3.8 kJ) gave 9 mm/s at 2.5 m and 0.2 mm/s at 60 m. Fall-off r^-1.28, about 25 Hz, with the site's Vs and damping table.
- **Fall-off exponents.** About 1.0 on sand to 1.6 on clay for drop tests (Auersch); 0.5–2.0 across source types (Auersch & Said).
- **Wave energy split (Miller & Pursey).** Radiated power Π = 1.209 ω²P²/(π ρ c_p³), split 67.35% Rayleigh, 25.76% shear, 6.89% compressional, via Sánchez-Sesma et al. Caltrans and TRL confirm the two-thirds Rayleigh share.
- **Caltrans 2020 tables.** Geometric spreading exponents (Rayleigh waves r^-0.5). Bornitz damping form and soil damping α values; three metric entries in that table have decimal slips. Woods & Jedele n by soil class. Construction vibration is typically 10–30 Hz.
- **Frequencies and wave speeds from abstracts.** Elephant footfall signals travel at 248–264 m/s at about 20 Hz (PubMed abstract). Footstep ground velocity is mostly 20–90 Hz (Ekimov & Sabatier, PubMed abstract).
- **Shear-wave speeds.** NEHRP Vs30 classes (USGS OFR 97-501); Turkish code Vs by soil type; ice Vs 1,934–1,960 m/s (Kohnen 1974).
- **Building amplification.** Low-rise houses amplify ground shaking 2–4× (Lauzon et al.).

What I derived and checked numerically:
- **An exact elastic-ground (Lamb's problem) model.** Near field: (1−ν)Ḟ/(2π ρ c_s² r). Far field (Rayleigh wave): 0.0763 F ω^1.5/(ρ c_s^2.5 √r). Its Rayleigh power matches Miller & Pursey to 0.02%.
- **Pekeris step-load formula.** I rebuilt the closed form from memory and checked it against an independent numerical calculation: they agree to 0.5–1.3% RMS.
- **Closed-form PPV for smooth force pulses.** Covers a stamp (pulse) and a footstep (rising load), plus a damping factor for pulses; constants and tables are in the facts.
- **Check against Pisa.** With plausible inputs the model is within 1.3× at 2.5–60 m; over the plausible range of contact times it is within about 2×.
- **Extrapolation.** The empirical laws are 100–100,000× out of their energy range for footsteps. At 3.8 kJ they still match near the drop but are 5× low at 60 m.

MODEL: Use the elastic-ground pulse model for the Walk tab (vib_physics_pulse_ppv_model). Do not use the dynamic-compaction energy laws as the main model: a walking step is a load that rises over 0.1–0.3 s with no free fall, so those laws have no clear input.

INPUTS per footfall: peak vertical force F (N) and one time scale.
- Walking: rise time τ, for a load that ramps up and is held. Toe-off unloading is a second, similar event; take the larger.
- Stamp or impact: pulse length T (Hann shape).
- Ground: ρ, c_s, ξ from vib_ground_wave_params_by_surface. Distance r in m.
- The forces and rise times should come from the step-force topic. The engine's stomp model can supply a stamp's F and contact time.

FORMULAS (Poisson ratio 1/4; c_R = 0.919 c_s):
- Hann pulse:
  - near = 0.375 F / (ρ c_s² T r)
  - far = 0.620 F / (ρ c_s^2.5 T^1.5 √r)
  - D = (1 + (ζ/0.224)^1.4)^(−2.5/1.4)
- Ramp:
  - near = 0.1875 F / (ρ c_s² τ r)
  - far = 0.181 F / (ρ c_s^2.5 τ^1.5 √r)
  - D = (1 + (ζ/0.195)^2.05)^(−1.5/2.05)
- ζ = ξ r / (0.919 c_s T), with τ in place of T for the ramp.
- PPV_vertical = max(near, far) × D. This is within 17% of the exact elastic result. Resultant (vector) PPV ≈ 1.1 × vertical.
- General near-field form for any smooth force history: 0.119 (dF/dt)_max / (ρ c_s² r).
- Dominant frequency to display: about 1/T for a pulse, 1/(2τ) to 1/τ for a ramp.

The formula is monotone: PPV rises with F and falls with T, τ, r, c_s, ρ and ξ.

ENERGY LINK: for the quake output use E_seismic = 0.148 ∫(dF/dt)² dt / (ρ c_s³), not 10⁻⁴ × energy. Example: a normal Rex step on loose sand radiates about 1.7 J.

VALIDITY:
- Distance from about 1 m (and more than 2 footprint radii) to 200 m.
- Footprint radius below 0.2 c_s T.
- Linear ground: PPV / c_s < 10⁻⁴, i.e. about 12 mm/s on loose sand and 25 mm/s on stiff clay. Flag or cap above that.
- Homogeneous ground; vertical force; one foot. With both feet in walking, the far field may be up to 2× lower.

IGNORES: soil yielding under the foot, layering and groundwater (dispersion), stiffening by slab or pavement, and building amplification (2–4× indoors).

UNCERTAINTY:
- About ×1.5–2 when the force history is known (Pisa check).
- About a factor of 3 either way on soil in general: c_s ±40%, rise time ±50%, Poisson ratio, damping.
- Larger beyond 100 m, where damping takes over, and on granite, where nothing was checked.

TESTS:
- Near-field limit: PPV·r → 0.375 F/(ρ c_s² T) for a Hann pulse.
- Far-field asymptotes: 0.620/√x and 0.181/√x.
- Pisa case: F = 2 × 997.4 / 0.03 N, T = 0.03 s, ρ = 1850, c_s = 150, ξ = 0.03 should give 9.3 / 6.1 / 0.65 / 0.21 mm/s at 2.5 / 5 / 35 / 60 m. Measured: 9 / 5 / 0.5 / 0.2.
- Monotonicity in effort and distance.
